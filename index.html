An editorial publication

We are living in the age of AI. Somebody should cover it properly.

The era has a name. It deserves a publication that covers it as an era — not as a gadget beat, not as hype, but as the transformation it is: agents that act, authority that moves, institutions that have to answer for both.

Covered through one lens

Every story in this publication asks the same two questions, because they are the questions the era turns on: who acted, and who authorized it? Gadget news doesn’t survive that lens. Power stories do.

Department I

Agents & Authority

What agents did, who authorized them, where the authorization broke, and what happened next. The core beat: formal authority vs. effective authority, reported from the incident, not the press release.

Department II

Work & Institutions

How jobs, firms, courts, and governments change when action can be delegated to systems — and what happens to accountability when the actor isn’t a person.

Department III

The Fail Log

A standing record of AI failures in the news, kept straight: what happened, what was authorized, what broke. The era’s mistakes, filed where they can be learned from instead of scrolled past.

Department IV

The Long View

History, not just headlines. The age of AI in the line of earlier ages — what rhymes, what doesn’t, and what the people who lived through the last transformation wish they’d known sooner.

How we cover it

Agency first

Every story starts with the act and the authorization. If a piece can’t say who acted and under what grant, it isn’t ready.

Evidence over hype

No gadget news, no press-release rewriting. Claims get checked against the record — the incident, the filing, the log — before they get printed.

The era, not the cycle

News cycles forget by Friday. Eras don’t. The publication is built to be re-read in five years and still hold up.
